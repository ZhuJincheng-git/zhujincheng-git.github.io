# Solidification

People often solidify without realizing it. Once life has largely settled, things revolve around you and you revolve around things; seeing the same things day after day, you gradually lose the ability to look at problems from a new perspective. I am afraid of this from the bottom of my heart—in my view, life should flow like water, and to stop moving is to die.

If solidification were only about preserving life's certainty at the cost of losing possibility, perhaps that would still be acceptable; what is most terrifying is when the heart dies too, becoming an Agent that no longer trains, a policy that no longer updates, like a leaf severed from its branch, withered away.

A severed leaf withers because it is severed from the branch; water dries up because it is cut off from rivers and seas. In this sense, ensuring that one remains in a suitable environment is extremely important. I cannot help but think that those who have solidified are precisely those living in rigid environments. If solidification is passively accepted, perhaps it is because life has already shown its end; if it is actively chosen, perhaps it is the indulgent pursuit of stability after a long rush. It is hard to say whether it is right or wrong.

*To assess a person's risk, do not look at what they lack, but at what will make them stop. I fear solidification, perhaps because I do not want to admit how small a person is, or perhaps because I have not yet lived enough and do not want to stop.*
